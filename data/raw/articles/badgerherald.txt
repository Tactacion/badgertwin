UW campus cooling issues lead to closure of multiple campus buildings
A break in a chilled water supply line leads to cooling issues on campus, with long-term repair expected to take a month or longer
Categories:
News
UW–Madison Campus
by Catalina DeSua
June 30, 2026
Shelly Zhang
Bascom Hall. April 27, 2025.
The UW-Madison campus experienced a significant break in a chilled water main supply line at one of the three heating and cooling plants that serve campus on June 17, according to an email sent to students, faculty and staff Friday. The break has caused campus buildings to have reduced cooling capacities this week, according to the email from UW Vice Chancellor for Finance and Administration Robert Cramer.
UW recognizes that temperatures are higher this week, but said that campus will remain open for business, according to the email. Facilities, Planning and Management crews have been working on short-term solutions to mitigate the effect of the heat, including the installation of approximately 30 temporary chillers across the campus, according to an FAQ website regarding cooling on campus.

Long-term repair is expected to take one month or more, and starting this week, over 20 buildings on campus will be temporarily closed to the public, according to the website. Some will be open but with limited cooling, and the university is prioritizing allocating cooling capacity to “critical areas such as patient care, research, and mitigating impacts to in-person instruction,” according to the email.
The email to students, faculty and staff  sent Monday recommends that the UW-Madison campus community engage in energy-saving habits, such as turning off lights and keeping windows closed, and that individuals attempt to resolve specific questions regarding the issue at the local level with a supervisor or manager.
