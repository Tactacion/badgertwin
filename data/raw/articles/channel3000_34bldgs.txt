MADISON, Wis. — More than 30 University of Wisconsin-Madison buildings are experiencing higher-than-normal temperatures and humidity because of a broken chilled water line that provides air conditioning, university officials said.
The university announced Friday that the broken line was significantly limiting cooling capacity across campus. As a result, some summer classes have been relocated.
The university said 34 buildings will be warmer than usual. Some will be temporarily closed to the public and will not be cooled at all.
Crews have been working on short-term mitigation measures while planning a long-term fix. Limited cooling capacity is being directed to UW Hospital and UW Health.
In a Sunday morning statement, Vice Chancellor for Finance and Administration Robert Cramer said repairs are expected to take at least one month.
“We recognize that this is a difficult situation and we’re doing everything we can to resolve it quickly,” Cramer said. “Part of what makes UW-Madison great is our history of coming together to find solutions that allow us to continue to fulfill our mission, and I have no doubt we will do so again.”
Campus will remain open for classes, normal business and work.

The First Warn Weather team has been tracking the heat dome and has called an ALERT DAY through Friday.
A summer camp counselor said staff had been warned that some indoor spaces could become hot. The counselor said Bakke Recreation & Wellbeing Center offered some relief because of its ice rink.
“My manager said it could be pretty hot, especially indoors, but fortunately, Bakke [Recreation and Wellbeing Center] has an ice rink,” the counselor said. “Sometimes, I just need to take kids into the ice rink to cool down a little bit.”
UW-Madison crews have secured and are installing about 30 temporary chillers across campus. The university said there will still be a shortfall from normal cooling capacity until a permanent new chilled water line can be installed.
