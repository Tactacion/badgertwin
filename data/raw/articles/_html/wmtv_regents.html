Board of Regents approved millions for aging infrastructure weeks before UW-Madison AC issues, records show

By Nicole Herzog
Published: Jun. 30, 2026 at 7:09 PM CDT|Updated: Jun. 30, 2026 at 7:25 PM CDT
Add Us On GoogleAdd as a preferred source on Google

MADISON, Wis. (WMTV) - More than 20 buildings on the University of Wisconsin-Madison campus are locked on Tuesday as crews work to address air conditioning issues caused by a broken chilled water line.

Meanwhile, university records are raising new questions about the state of UW-Madison’s infrastructure.

Twenty-three buildings are fully closed to the public, including Bascom Hall, Science Hall and Grainger Hall. Eleven more are partially open.

The university says repairs will take at least a month.

The university said impacted buildings will remain temporarily closed even after the current heat wave ends while repairs continue.

Buildings not under closure are still experiencing higher than normal temperatures and humidity, according to an update from the university on Tuesday.

University officials are asking people on campus to conserve energy by turning off lights, keeping blinds and windows closed and limiting entries and exits from buildings.

Officials are also asking people not to bring in window or personal air conditioning units and to turn off heat-generating equipment that is not in use.

Prior infrastructure concerns documented

Records show the current cooling problems are not the first sign of infrastructure issues at UW-Madison.

On June 4, the Board of Regents approved nearly $3 million to repair a chilled water system in the Engineering Centers Building.

“Recently, a bolt failed on a mechanical fitting in the penthouse causing a flood into valuable research space in the building,” meeting minutes state. “The entire building was closed for nearly two weeks, interrupting class schedules and research. Welding of all the chilled water piping fittings, as proposed in this project, will eliminate the risk of future flooding into research space.”

Those same documents show a separate campus chiller — more than 40 years old — had already failed and was running on a rented temporary unit before this week’s issues arose. Nearly $3 million more was approved to replace the chiller system at the Charmany Farms Veterinary Medicine Building.

“One of the two chillers has failed catastrophically and has been replaced by a rented chiller set up with temporary piping and hoses,” the document states. “All cooling equipment has reached the end of its useful life and needs replacement.”

It is unclear whether construction on either of those projects has started.

UW-Madison declined requests for an on-camera interview and did not provide a clear response to specific questions.

A Universities of Wisconsin spokesperson provided a written statement, writing:

The complex physical infrastructure of our universities is both functional and aging, and it requires regular maintenance and oversight. We prioritize repairs and improvements based on safety, operational need, and available funding, working closely with state partners to make the most of limited resources so our universities can continue to serve students and support Wisconsin’s workforce and economy for years to come.

Mark Pitsch, Universities of Wisconsin Director of Media Relations

Campus is still operating as normal. Both unions are open, but the university said food service at Memorial Union may be more limited than usual.
