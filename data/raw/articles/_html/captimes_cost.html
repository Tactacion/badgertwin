UW-Madison's summer air conditioning woes cost $15.8 million

On Oct. 8, the University of Wisconsin-Madison revealed the cost of its air conditioning problems over the summer: $15.8 million.

A major chilled water line ruptured in June near the Charter Street Healing and Cooling Plant. The pipe was 4 feet in diameter and buried 20 feet underground, and was part of a system that provides air conditioning for much of campus.

The repairs for the chilled water line were about $1.8 million, and the temporary chillers used to cool buildings cost roughly $10 million, Lori Wilson, a spokesperson for the university's Division of Facilities Planning and Management, told the Cap Times in an email. Work to address additional deterioration in the area will cost about $4 million.

UW-Madison and the state continue to investigate what caused the break in June, Wilson said.

To help with these expenses and additional repairs, members of the UW system’s Board of Regents voted Oct. 8 to reallocate $40 million to expand the budget for a related UW-Madison utilities project on Engineering Drive.
